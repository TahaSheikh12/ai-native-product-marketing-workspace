# Origin and evolution

## The starting point

The original inspiration was an internal Cursor onboarding workspace created by a senior leader. Its central idea was powerful: an AI assistant becomes substantially more useful when its rules, knowledge, reusable methods and state live together in a portable folder.

I did not create that original onboarding system.

## The adaptation

I recognised that the same architectural pattern could support recurring product marketing work. I templatized what I had learned and created a product-marketing-focused version built around real PMM decisions rather than generic prompting.

The working version was used for approximately five months. Four colleagues adopted it, providing a practical signal that the structure was understandable and transferable beyond its creator.

## The migration

When the enterprise Cursor licence was withdrawn, I migrated the workspace to Claude Code. The migration reinforced a key design principle: the valuable asset was not a specific AI interface. It was the operating knowledge captured in files, rules, context and reusable methods.

## The public reconstruction

This repository is a new, clean reconstruction designed for public distribution. It:

- focuses exclusively on product marketing;
- uses a MECE lifecycle of five workflows;
- separates workflows from eight analytical skills;
- adds evidence and claims governance;
- supports Cursor, Claude Code and Codex from one instruction source;
- excludes employer material, customer information and the original private scripts;
- avoids fictional performance claims or manufactured case studies.

## What I contributed

- Recognising that the original pattern could solve a different functional problem.
- Translating it into a PMM operating model.
- Using it over time and refining it through actual work.
- Enabling adoption by four colleagues.
- Preserving the system through a platform migration.
- Rebuilding the public version with clearer analytical boundaries, governance and portability.

The project is therefore not presented as invention of the original concept. It demonstrates adaptation, product thinking, operational design, adoption and AI fluency in practice.
