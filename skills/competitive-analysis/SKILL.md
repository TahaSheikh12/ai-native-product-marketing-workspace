# Competitive analysis

Explain how relevant alternatives differ in the context of a buyer decision without inventing claims or reducing the work to a feature checklist.

## MECE boundary

- **Unit of analysis:** a named competitor, substitute or status quo alternative.
- **Owns:** alternative profiles, decision-criteria comparison and defensible differentiation.
- **Does not own:** opportunity win rates, general market attractiveness or unsupported battlecard claims.

## Establish the analysis

Define the audience, use case, buying stage, alternatives in scope, evidence date and decision the analysis must inform.

## Analysis

1. Include direct competitors, substitutes and no-change alternatives.
2. Build a source register and label vendor claims, customer evidence and inference separately.
3. Compare alternatives against buyer decision criteria rather than feature count.
4. Identify where each alternative is strong, weak or a better fit.
5. Distinguish meaningful differentiation from parity and unproven claims.
6. Map implications to positioning, discovery questions, objection handling or product gaps.
7. Flag stale evidence and claims requiring legal or product validation.

## Output

- Scope, audience and alternatives
- Buyer decision criteria
- Evidence-backed comparison
- Strengths, weaknesses and best-fit situations
- Differentiation and parity
- Field implications and discovery questions
- Evidence gaps and refresh date
