# Pricing and packaging analysis

Structure pricing and packaging evidence so stakeholders can make transparent offer decisions while recognising finance, product, legal and sales dependencies.

## MECE boundary

- **Unit of analysis:** an offer, package, value metric or price point.
- **Owns:** packaging logic, value-metric fit, willingness-to-pay evidence and scenario comparison.
- **Does not own:** final pricing authority, account prioritisation, revenue recognition or financial forecasting.

## Establish the analysis

Define the customer segment, use case, geography, currency, purchase motion, commercial constraints and decision required.

## Analysis

1. Map customer value, usage and cost-to-serve drivers.
2. Evaluate possible value metrics for alignment, predictability, measurability and customer control.
3. Compare package boundaries with needs, complexity and adoption paths.
4. Separate observed willingness-to-pay evidence from stated preference.
5. Analyse current price realisation, discounting and migration risk when data exists.
6. Build transparent scenarios for price, package and migration choices.
7. Consider channel, contract, legal, operational and customer-experience constraints.
8. Recommend tests and decision gates before broad rollout.

## Safeguards

- Do not infer willingness to pay from product usage alone.
- Do not use a simple average across non-comparable segments or currencies.
- Do not hide cannibalisation, migration or grandfathering assumptions.
- Treat survey methods and small samples as directional unless designed for inference.

## Output

- Decision, audience and scope
- Value and willingness-to-pay evidence
- Value-metric assessment
- Package and price scenarios
- Customer, revenue and operational trade-offs
- Migration risks and guardrails
- Recommended test and approval path
