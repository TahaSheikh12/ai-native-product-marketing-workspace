# Segment opportunity analysis

Prioritise where to play using consistent evidence about customer need, market attractiveness, strategic fit and ability to win.

## MECE boundary

- **Unit of analysis:** an external market, customer segment or use case.
- **Owns:** segmentation, attractiveness and priority comparisons.
- **Does not own:** internal revenue mix, individual accounts, positioning copy or offer pricing.

## Establish the analysis

Define the decision, segmentation basis, geography, time horizon and minimum evidence standard. Keep segment definition separate from scoring criteria.

## Analysis

1. Create segments that are identifiable, meaningfully different and actionable.
2. Assess customer need, urgency and consequences of inaction.
3. Estimate size or potential using a transparent method and range.
4. Assess growth, accessibility, strategic fit, competitive intensity and right to win.
5. Use consistent criteria and show raw evidence alongside any weights.
6. Test sensitivity to uncertain assumptions and weights.
7. Identify poor-fit segments and prerequisites.
8. Recommend a priority, validation step or decision to defer.

## Safeguards

- Do not confuse a large market with an attractive opportunity.
- Do not double count nested segments.
- Keep TAM, serviceable opportunity and obtainable opportunity distinct.
- Treat scoring as decision support, not objective truth.

## Output

- Segment definitions and scope
- Criteria, evidence and assumptions
- Comparative opportunity table
- Sensitivity and confidence
- Priority and deprioritised segments
- Risks, prerequisites and next validation step
