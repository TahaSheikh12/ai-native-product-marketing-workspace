# Win/loss analysis

Explain commercial outcomes well enough to guide positioning, product, enablement or go-to-market decisions without overstating what observational deal data proves.

## MECE boundary

- **Unit of analysis:** a qualified commercial opportunity and its outcome.
- **Owns:** win rate, loss/no-decision patterns and opportunity-level reasons.
- **Does not own:** general competitor profiling, program attribution or individual seller performance management.

## Minimum useful data

- Opportunity identifier
- Final outcome and close date
- Opportunity value and currency
- Product or solution
- Customer segment, region and relevant cohort dimensions
- Sales stage reached
- Competitor or alternative where known
- Recorded reason codes

Useful additions include cycle length, source, seller, use case, pricing, discount, notes and buyer interviews.

## Prepare the dataset

1. Define the analysis period and eligible opportunity population.
2. Separate won, competitor loss, no decision, delayed, disqualified and administratively closed outcomes.
3. Standardise currencies, products, segments, competitors and reason taxonomies.
4. Remove duplicates and document reopened or split opportunities.
5. Flag small cohorts, high missingness and seller-entered fields with inconsistent definitions.

## Quantitative analysis

- Report win rate by opportunity count and value.
- State the denominator explicitly; do not include disqualified or administrative closures without justification.
- Compare outcome, deal size and cycle time by product, segment, region, use case, source and competitor where sample size permits.
- Show counts alongside percentages.
- Compare time periods carefully for changes in cohort mix.
- Identify stage exits and no-decision patterns separately from competitive losses.
- Use confidence intervals or clear small-sample warnings when differences may be unstable.

## Qualitative analysis

1. Keep seller-selected CRM reasons separate from buyer-verified reasons.
2. Code notes or interviews with a documented taxonomy.
3. Allow multiple contributing reasons rather than forcing one cause.
4. Preserve representative evidence without exposing confidential customer details.
5. Identify contradictions between quantitative patterns and stated reasons.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Outcome differences by seller, segment or competitor do not prove causation.
- Late-stage losses may be overrepresented in interview samples.
- CRM loss reasons often reflect seller interpretation or required-field behaviour.
- Win rate can improve because opportunity qualification changed, not because market performance improved.
- A competitor named in the CRM may not be the buyer's true alternative; no decision and status quo matter.
- Do not rank individuals or recommend employment action from this analysis.

## Output

Provide:

- Scope, population and taxonomy
- Data-quality and sample limitations
- Overall win rate by count and value
- Cohort cuts with counts and confidence warnings
- Competitive, no-decision and funnel findings
- Quantitative and qualitative reason analysis kept visibly distinct
- Implications for positioning, product, enablement and sales process
- Prioritised actions, each with an owner, proposed test and success measure
- Questions requiring buyer research or additional data

Recommendations must connect to an observed pattern and propose a way to validate the interpretation.
